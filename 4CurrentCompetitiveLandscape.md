Strategic White Space & Patent Augmentation Opportunities
What is Strategic White Space?
White space refers to technical areas where no existing patents create barriers — combinations of targets, formats, or mechanisms that are either unclaimed or only weakly covered by competitors. Filing in white space gives ABL Bio freedom to operate and builds a defensive portfolio that is difficult for competitors to design around.
Key Observations from the Landscape
The competitive landscape is heavily concentrated in Topo1 (110 patents) and B7H3 (34 patents) as single-target ADCs, reflecting the commercial success of approved Topo1-payload ADCs like trastuzumab deruxtecan. ROR1 remains comparatively sparse with only 11 single-target patents, representing a less crowded space. Critically, multi-target combinations — particularly ROR1+B7H3 and ROR1+Topo1 — each have only 5 patents, and true tri-target coverage (ROR1+B7H3+Topo1) exists in only 4 patents globally. Bispecific combinations remain nearly unexplored with only 4 patents total across all target combinations.
Augmentation Strategies
ABL Bio can pursue several layers of patent augmentation without requiring entirely new biological discoveries:
Continuation & CIP Applications — Filing continuation-in-part applications on existing families to add new data (e.g., new tumor models, patient subpopulations, combination therapies) extends patent term and broadens claim scope without starting from scratch.
Linker & Payload Modifications — The current landscape does not distinguish between cleavable vs. non-cleavable linkers, or between payload classes (MMAE, DM1, SN-38, PBD). Filing on specific linker-payload combinations tied to ABL Bio's antibody scaffolds creates layered protection that is difficult to design around even if the target claim is challenged.
Bispecific Format — With only 4 bispecific competitor patents across all targets, the bispecific ADC format represents the clearest white space. ABL Bio's Grabody-B platform is particularly well positioned to claim novel bispecific ADC architectures combining ROR1 or B7H3 with a tumor microenvironment target.
Method of Treatment Claims — Even where composition claims are crowded, method claims covering specific indications, dosing regimens, or patient selection biomarkers remain relatively open and provide independent patent protection.
Formulation & Manufacturing — Downstream patents on specific formulations, conjugation chemistries, or manufacturing processes provide a final layer of protection that persists even after core composition patents expire.
